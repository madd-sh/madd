# The MADD Community

MADD (Multi-Agent Driven Development) is built in the open. This page explains where things happen and how to take part, whether you want to ask a question, share an experience, or help the method evolve.

> Honest expectation: MADD is maintained part-time. This community is **asynchronous and contributor-driven by design**. You do not need to wait for the maintainer to start a discussion, answer a peer, or propose a change. The best ideas here will come from people using MADD on real projects, not from a central authority.

## Where things happen

| You want to... | Go to |
|----------------|-------|
| Ask a question, share a retro-spec, swap experiences | [Discussions](https://github.com/madd-sh/madd/discussions) |
| Report a bug in the `madd` CLI or a doc error | [Issues](https://github.com/madd-sh/madd/issues) |
| Propose a concrete change (code, skills, docs) | [Pull Requests](https://github.com/madd-sh/madd/pulls) |
| Read the full methodology | [madd.sh](https://madd.sh) |

## Discussion categories

- **Announcements** - releases and notable changes (maintainer-posted).
- **Ideas / RFC** - propose an evolution of the method or the tooling. This is where the method changes start.
- **Q&A** - ask anything about applying MADD. Answers from peers are as valuable as answers from maintainers; mark the one that helped.
- **Show & tell** - case studies, retro-specs, wins and failures. Failures are as useful as successes.
- **General** - everything else.

## How the method evolves: lightweight RFCs

MADD is a methodology, so the most important contributions change *the method itself*, not just the code. The flow is intentionally simple:

1. **Open an "Ideas / RFC" discussion** describing the problem and your proposed change. Keep it concrete: what breaks today, what you would change, what the tradeoff is.
2. **Let it breathe.** Others react, push back, refine. Rough consensus in the thread is the signal, not a maintainer veto.
3. **Open a Pull Request** that implements the change (in the method docs, the skills, or the CLI). Link it back to the discussion.
4. **Merge** once the change is coherent and the discussion supports it.

This is how the method stays anchored in real use instead of one person's opinion.

## Sharing a case study

Applied MADD on something real? That is the highest-value contribution you can make. Post it in **Show & tell** with: context, what you did, what worked, what did not, and any numbers you can share. A sanitized retro-spec is gold. You can also open a Pull Request to add it to the case studies on the site.

## Good first ways to contribute

- Answer an open **Q&A** thread from your own experience.
- Add a missing entry to the [FAQ](docs/FAQ.md).
- Report a friction point you hit while applying MADD as an **Ideas / RFC** discussion.
- Improve a skill or a doc and open a Pull Request.

## Conduct
